Who's behind Cliick

We wanted this for our own lives.

Cliick isn't a business plan looking for a market. It's two people building the thing we wished existed — somewhere to keep up with our own families, our own friends, our own ordinary days, without handing all of it to an advertising platform.

You should know who is asking for your trust.

Don't take our word for it.

Most privacy promises are policy, and policy changes with a funding round. We would rather it be structural. So here is everything Cliick holds about you — and everything it has nowhere to put.

What we hold

  • A display name, up to 30 characters
  • A profile photo, a color, a mascot
  • Which sign‑in you used, as an opaque id
  • Your cliicks, and what you have shared in them

What there is no column for

  • Your email address
  • Your phone number
  • Your contacts
  • Your mailing address
  • Your card

When you sign in, we ask Apple for no scopes at all and Google for exactly one: openid. Your email address never reaches us, so we could not email you if we wanted to. Membership runs through the App Store and Google Play, so your card never touches us either.

Not a platform. A place.

We are not trying to build the next big social network. There is no audience to grow here, no reach to chase, and nothing to optimize for except whether the people you invited are glad they came.

We love the public internet and we are not asking you to leave it. Keep watching, shopping, learning, following. This is for the other part — the part that was never meant to be content.

Sometimes the change you’re waiting for is the one you have to build.

Nina & John

Cliick started as a conversation between the two of us about where our own family photos ought to live. It is self‑funded: no investors, and no funding round waiting to change the terms. A few volunteers help out because they believe in it.
